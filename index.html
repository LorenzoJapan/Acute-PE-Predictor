About

Two pathways to pretest probability

The home screen offers two independent methods for reaching pretest probability. Whichever is used, probability then routes through the same PERC / D-dimer / imaging strategy described below, following Figure 1 of the 2026 AHA/ACC acute PE guideline.1,6

Wells Expanded Criteria Pathway

A three-step structured assessment — cardiopulmonary symptoms, vitals/exam/CXR/EKG findings, and a clinical judgment of whether an alternative diagnosis is more, equally, or less likely than PE — classifies the presentation as typical, atypical, or severe, then combines that with VTE risk factors (prior DVT/PE, recent immobilization or surgery, malignancy, estrogen use) to reach a Low, Moderate, or High pretest probability. It draws on the same elements as the classic Wells criteria — signs of DVT, heart rate, immobilization/surgery, prior VTE, hemoptysis, malignancy, and whether an alternative diagnosis is less likely — expanded into a stepwise exam rather than a single point score.1

Clinical Gestalt Pathway

Skips the structured steps entirely — select Low (<15%), Intermediate (15–50%), or High (>50%) directly, based on overall clinical impression.

Low probability — PERC rule-out

When pretest probability is low, the pulmonary embolism rule-out criteria (PERC) are screened next: age <50y, HR <100, SpO₂ ≥95% on room air, no hemoptysis, no unilateral leg swelling, no prior DVT/PE, no exogenous estrogen use, and no surgery or trauma requiring hospitalization/general anesthesia in the past 4 weeks. If all 8 are negative, PE is excluded without D-dimer testing or imaging. Any positive item routes to the same YEARS-adjusted D-dimer step used at intermediate pretest probability, described below.3 On the Wells Expanded Criteria Pathway, items already captured during the symptoms/vitals/exam steps are carried over rather than asked twice; on the Clinical Gestalt Pathway all eight are asked fresh, since nothing is captured up front.

YEARS-adjusted D-dimer threshold

Low pretest probability that is PERC-positive, and intermediate pretest probability, both route to the same D-dimer step,2,6 with the threshold set using three YEARS items — clinical signs of DVT, hemoptysis, and PE being the most likely diagnosis. None present: <1000 ng/mL rules out PE. One or more present: <500 ng/mL rules out PE.4 On the Wells Expanded Criteria Pathway these are read directly from the earlier steps; on the Clinical Gestalt Pathway they're asked fresh at the D-dimer step.

High probability — hemodynamic instability

When pretest probability is high and shock criteria are met (SBP <90 mmHg with tachycardia, mechanical ventilation, or a high oxygen requirement), the recommendation adds bedside transthoracic echocardiography (TTE) as an alternative to CTPA if the patient cannot be safely transported. Right ventricular strain/dysfunction on TTE in this context supports emergency reperfusion therapy without waiting for CTPA confirmation.5
